# Afaan Oromo mathematics textbook review

Date: 30 September 2026

## Sources and review scope

User supplied MathSBG7.pdf and MathSBG8.pdf, both titled *Herrega Kitaaba Barattootaa*, published by Biiroo Barnootaa Oromiyaa in 2014 E.C./2022, with Asalla teacher education college acknowledged. Grade 7 has 213 numbered instructional pages; Grade 8 has 226 numbered pages including numerical tables. Front matter and blank pages are additional.

The full text of both PDFs was extracted into adjacent text files. I reviewed the contents and objectives of all 15 chapters, selected introductory, middle and review pages in every chapter, activity/group-work passages throughout both books, and additional procedures and examples around arithmetic, fractions, percentages and algebra. Selected original PDF pages were rendered to verify diagrams and a suspected numerical inconsistency. This is an extensive structural and example review, not a claim that every exercise and diagram has been individually translated or checked.

Page references below are the books' printed page numbers, not PDF viewer numbers. Some equations, comparison signs, set symbols, superscripts and diagram labels disappear or become detached in text extraction. Equation authoring must use the original PDF, not extracted text alone.

Google Translate was tested but its domains returned proxy HTTP 403. Translation access was added to the saved network draft, preserving existing domains. No Google translation results were obtained and no translation integration was implemented. English terminology below is a working interpretation of the mathematical context; learner-facing Afaan Oromo wording requires the user's review.

These 2022 editions establish the content of the supplied books. They do not establish the official 2019 E.C./2026–27 exam syllabus, topic weights, question format or which editions are currently prescribed. No science or English textbook was supplied.

### Observations from Grade 7 activities and examples

- **Sets, pp. 2–10:** The book begins with familiar collections, including students, regions and letters. It distinguishes having the same number of members from having the same members. A learner must understand that equal size alone does not mean equal sets.
- **Arithmetic prerequisite evidence, pp. 20–23:** Review exercises already include multi-digit addition, ordering numbers and number sequences. This is review, not an elementary arithmetic course.
- **Integers, Gocha 2.2, p. 24:** A learner has 15 birr and bananas cost 5 birr each. Questions ask about buying two, three and four bananas. The ensuing discussion introduces a shortage/borrowing interpretation for negative numbers. This connects arithmetic to the need for numbers below zero.
- **Number-line activity, Gocha 2.5, p. 36:** Start at 1 and move three units right; start at 4 and move seven units left; start at −3 and move six units right. Learners then express the movement mathematically. The activity connects action, result and notation directly.
- **Multiplication/division, pp. 41–46:** Repeated addition introduces multiplication, followed by signed-number rules. Division is linked to finding a missing multiplication factor. The book explicitly states that division by zero is undefined and that division is not commutative or associative.
- **Ratios and money, pp. 54–78:** Ratios use classroom counts and everyday quantities. Exercises include unlike units that must be converted first. Profit/loss percentages use buying price as the base. Simple interest and compound interest are both present; neither should silently disappear from a coverage map.
- **Equation meaning, pp. 95–100 and Gocha 4.6, p. 116:** The book discusses preserving equality by doing the same operation on both sides. Two baskets initially containing seven apples each provide a concrete context. It also verifies solutions by substitution rather than treating an answer as sufficient.
- **Area reasoning, pp. 147–154:** Area is defined through unit squares. A parallelogram is cut/rearranged into a rectangle. A rectangle is divided into congruent triangles to obtain a triangle-area formula. Perimeter and area need separate understanding.
- **Congruence, p. 168 onward:** Learners overlay shapes and reason about matching size and shape. Later tasks require correspondence and justification; recognising a familiar picture alone is insufficient.
- **Statistics, pp. 193–213:** Pie charts require conversion between proportions and angles. Mean, median, mode and range are distinct. Review exercises also ask for an unknown datum from a specified mean or median, connecting statistics to algebra.

### Observations from Grade 8 activities and examples

- **Rational-number need, pp. 4–5:** 92 birr is allocated equally across five children. The result is 92/5 = 18.40 birr per child. The text uses a situation not solved within whole numbers to motivate rational numbers.
- **Representation, pp. 7–12:** Fractions are placed by subdividing number-line intervals equally. Absolute value is distance from zero, not simply a rule to remove a minus sign.
- **Comparisons, pp. 14–19:** Methods include equivalent fractions/common denominators and number lines. A table compares scores from tests with different totals; comparing raw marks alone would be misleading.
- **Fraction operations, pp. 20–35:** Rectangular partition models support addition and subtraction. Overlapping shading supports multiplication, including 1/4 × 1/2 = 1/8. Division is also represented as how many quarter-sized portions fit into a half. These are materially different interpretations, not interchangeable decorations.
- **Problem-solving process, pp. 37–39:** Understand the information, select a method, carry it out and verify. A 250,000 birr allocation is shown both with a partition model and arithmetic; the allocations plus the remainder must total the original amount.
- **Squares, Gocha 2.1, p. 48:** Nine rows of nine seedlings motivate squaring. Square roots later ask for a missing side or factor. Cube-root work connects to volume. The numerical-table and approximation procedures form part of the book's demands.
- **Graphs, p. 82:** Select x-values, calculate y-values, plot points and draw the line. This requires relationships between multiple representations, not just recognising an equation.
- **Inequalities, pp. 85–89:** The text asks learners to distinguish strict and inclusive bounds and examine competing solutions. Signed operations and the direction of an inequality must be understood.
- **Similarity, pp. 103–110:** Learners construct and measure figures. Side and perimeter ratios have scale factor k; area ratios have k². Doubling length does not merely double area.
- **Triangle angles, Hojii Garee 5.1, p. 118:** Learners draw a triangle, cut off its three corners, measure them and arrange them along a straight line. This is an action-first route to the 180-degree result.
- **Pythagoras, Hojii Garee 5.2, pp. 134–135:** Rearranging congruent right triangles and comparing areas leads to the theorem. Exercises include ladders and diagonals, not only direct substitution into a formula.
- **Circle angles, Gocha 6.5, p. 157:** Construct central and inscribed angles subtending the same arc, measure both and compare. The result connects to the inscribed-angle theorem.
- **Solid nets, pp. 180–188:** Learners unfold prism, cylinder, pyramid and cone surfaces to reason about area. A cylinder's curved surface unfolds to a rectangle whose width is the circumference. Surface area and volume need different models.
- **Volume, Gocha 7.7, p. 191:** Build a 5 × 4 × 3 rectangular prism, fill it with unit cubes, count, compare with multiplication, repeat with other dimensions and infer the formula. This passage was visually verified in the original PDF.
- **Probability, Gocha 8.3 and Hojii Garee 8.2, pp. 204–205:** Dice and coin activities generate and record outcomes. Chapter exercises progress to favourable outcomes divided by possible outcomes. Observed frequencies and theoretical probability must not be conflated.

## Consequences for the proposed app

These reading findings informed the implemented preview; they do not establish complete curriculum or current exam coverage.

1. Basic arithmetic is a prerequisite layer absent from these books' main progression. The app needs to discover and teach missing foundations while preserving the route back to the relevant Grade 7/8 objectives.
2. Textbook order and catch-up order need not be identical. For a struggling learner, proceeding through sets and formal notation before addressing arithmetic may obscure the actual blocker.
3. The user's requested action-to-definition sequence has genuine support in the books: move on a number line, cut and rearrange, shade equal parts, fill with unit cubes, measure, experiment, then state a rule.
4. A game must preserve the mathematical relationship. An unrelated action before a quiz does not reproduce the learning in these activities.
5. Learning must transfer to the representations used in the books: expressions, diagrams, comparison statements, unknowns, tables, constructed graphs, proofs/justifications and word problems. Activities cannot end at animation alone.
6. A thousand generated variations is a volume target, not evidence of a thousand distinct teaching methods or of exam readiness. Track objective, prerequisite, representation, misconception, difficulty and source reference for each variation.
7. English should support access gradually. The supplied maths books are Afaan Oromo; they do not establish an English literacy syllabus. English vocabulary must not become a prerequisite to understanding the mathematics.
8. For offline Android, reviewed text and recordings can be bundled. Live Google translation cannot be required during learning. Translation assistance during content preparation is separate from learner runtime requirements.

## Source issue confirmed visually

**Grade 7, p. 38, Fakkeenya 13:** The narrative specifies 116 m below sea level, while the diagram/table and solution use 120 m below sea level. The solution obtains 4,427 m from 4,307 − (−120). Using the narrative's 116 gives 4,423 m. Do not automatically turn this example into an answer key without resolving which value is intended. The inconsistency is in the PDF, not only extraction.

**Grade 8, p. 55:** the printed equality `(−5)^5 = (5)^2 = 25` has an incorrect first exponent; the worked square example requires `(−5)^2`. This was checked against the original PDF. The square-root lab uses independently verified examples instead.

Other missing operators or distorted exponents in extracted text have not been classified as textbook errors without checking the original page.
