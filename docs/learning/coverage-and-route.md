# Source coverage and prerequisite learning route

Review date: 30 September 2026. This document maps the current Flutter mathematics mechanisms to the supplied textbooks and proposes a practical catch-up route. It distinguishes executable interactions from the broader content blueprints. It does not claim complete chapter coverage or predict how quickly an individual learner will catch up.

## Evidence and count boundaries

The source books are *Herrega Kitaaba Barattootaa Kutaa 7* and *Kutaa 8*, Biiroo Barnootaa Oromiyaa, 2014 E.C./2022. References below are **printed page numbers**. The supplied Grade 7 PDF has 220 PDF pages, including front matter and a blank ending, and its instructional numbering ends at **213**. Grade 8 has instructional pages 1–221 and numerical tables at 222–226. The earlier 214 endpoint in reading notes was incorrect; the catalogue/JSON metadata and `g7d07` reference were corrected to 213.

I read the generator and transfer-copy implementation, the twelve board classes, the eight mathematics discovery-lab classes and one English recognition lab, their lesson records, and the progress/recommendation logic. The source snapshot includes all nine populated labs and their navigation from the home screen. This is a content/source review, not a fresh Flutter test or Android runtime report.

There are three separate quantities:

| Artefact | What its count means |
|---|---|
| Runtime generator in `lib/learning/scenarios.dart` | 12 `GameKind` mechanisms × 100 deterministic numerical configurations = **1,200 generated rounds**. These are variations within twelve representations, not 1,200 different instructional methods. |
| `lib/learning/discoveries.dart` and `lib/labs/` | **Eight fixed mathematics discoveries plus one English printed-word recognition lab**, each with its own transfer. These nine activities are separate from the generated 1,200 rounds. |
| `docs/learning/blueprints.json` | **89 content blueprints**, with 712 parameter configurations and **1,424 build/repair scenario design slots**. These are specifications, not an additional 1,424 runtime screens. |

Do not add these numbers and call the result a count of distinct methods or fully implemented lessons. A board can support part of a blueprint without implementing all its actions, misconception handling or transfer demands.

## What the twelve game mechanisms currently teach

| `GameKind` / board | Observable interaction and existing transfer | Boundary of the implementation |
|---|---|---|
| `numberLine` / `NumberLineBoard` | Move an integer position by ±1 until a stated addition/subtraction target is reached. Transfer changes the starting value. Generated cases progress from positive additions to negative movements and negative starting positions. | No place-value bundling, carrying/borrowing, rational ticks, explicit ordering assessment, zero-pair counters, or subtraction-of-a-negative construction. Arriving at a position alone does not demonstrate why a signed rule works. |
| `equalGroups` / `EqualGroupsBoard` | Add/remove counters so every group has the requested equal count; connect groups × count to a total. Transfer adds one group. | Positive integers from 1 to 10 only. No distributive array cut, multi-digit written method, commutativity explanation or general signed multiplication. The signed-products discovery supplies one additional case. |
| `sharing` / `SharingBoard` | Allocate a fixed supply among people and redistribute until all receive equal shares. Transfer adds one complete round of shares. | All generated totals divide exactly into positive integer shares. No grouping-division comparison, remainders, fractional shares, signed quotient or division-by-zero exploration. |
| `fractionTiles` / `FractionTilesBoard` | Shade a requested proper fraction of pre-made equal tiles; name numerator/denominator visually. Transfer asks for the new denominator. | Basic fraction representation. It does not assess equivalence, size comparison, addition, multiplication or general division. The division discovery adds one positive measurement case. |
| `balance` / `BalanceBoard` | Change the candidate value of x and compare `ax+b` with the target. Transfer changes an equation to another positive integer solution. | This is substitution/search with a balance display. It does **not** let the learner apply the same operation to both sides, manipulate terms/brackets, solve fractional coefficients or construct a graph. The hint supplies an inverse-operation expression. |
| `areaGrid` / `AreaGridBoard` | Cover each unit square in a rectangular array; transfer changes one dimension. | Rectangle area only. No perimeter task, height choice, cut/rearrange proof, triangle/trapezoid/circle area, composite plot or measurement conversion. |
| `angleBuilder` / `AngleBuilderBoard` | Adjust a third angle so three coloured angles form a 180° straight angle; transfer changes a known angle. | Triangle interior-angle sum illustration and calculation. No exterior angle, parallel-line proof, Euclidean projection, or general right-triangle theorem. The Pythagoras/circle discoveries extend different ideas. |
| `similarity` / `SimilarityBoard` | Resize both dimensions of a rectangle to the requested integer scale, with visual feedback on proportional shape. Transfer scales one new side. | One rectangle family with scale factors 2–5. This board has no rigid congruence overlay, vertex correspondence task, AA/SSS/SAS test, perimeter-ratio assessment or area-ratio `k²` transfer. The separate congruence discovery adds a rigid overlay and a scale distractor. |
| `volume` / `VolumeBoard` | Adjust a rectangular prism’s width/depth/layers; count base squares × number of layers and transfer to a new dimension. | Rectangular-prism volume only. No solid classification, net, surface-area covering, nonrectangular prism, cylinder or litre conversion. |
| `probability` / `ProbabilityBoard` | Inspect a red/blue bag, draw repeatedly, see empirical red counts, and set the favourable/total counts. Transfer asks the new total number of outcomes. | A single draw from equally likely individual tokens, with replacement across trials. No certain/impossible generator cases (both colours are always present), ordered combined sample space, dice-sum counting or assessment that separates theory from an empirical frequency. Success requires the raw counts; equivalent simplified probability fractions are not the accepted construction state. |
| `sets` / `SetsBoard` | Sort shape/colour tokens into A-only, shared, and B-only regions. Transfer counts a new union while counting shared items once. | All three generated regions are nonempty. No empty/disjoint/contained set case, subset relation, equal versus equinumerous sets, finite/infinite rule, or object/member versus collection notation task. |
| `data` / `DataBoard` | Move one unit between five stacks without changing their sum until all are equal. Transfer shifts every datum by one and asks for its mean. | Five nonnegative integer data values whose total is divisible by five. No fractional mean, variable count, frequency/pie chart, median, mode, range, missing datum or weighted combined mean. |

Existing transfers provide a useful bridge from an interaction to numerical notation. Several are close near-transfers—change one number and repeat the same structure. A full learning gate should also require a change of representation, an explanation and an unfamiliar contextual task. A single integer answer cannot test all those demands.

## Efficient prerequisite route, beginning with counting

The efficient route is to probe a small prerequisite, teach only the demonstrated gap, then return to the learner’s useful goal. Do not require every foundation to be repeated because one fraction answer is wrong. Do not skip a prerequisite because the next screen looks engaging. Grade 7 sets can run alongside arithmetic; it is not necessary to finish all formal set notation before repairing counting or equal parts.

Offer one short model at a time, first orally in reviewed Afaan Oromo with objects/pictures if needed. Follow with numerals and symbols, then short written questions. Keep the goal stable while changing representation. An explanation may be spoken or demonstrated; English sentences are not a gate for mathematics.

For each gate below, use **two previously unseen numerical cases**, a short explanation/representation check and a later unaided retry. The example answer is provided for the reviewer, not a value to reveal before checking. Gate thresholds are a design proposal, not a validated mastery scale.

This route deliberately connects harder work back to its mathematical blocker. It does not promise that games remove the need for practice, instruction, definitions, written work or later retrieval. A learner may show an intuitive model quickly while still needing several sessions to transfer to symbols, a changed context and textbook-style reasoning.

## Practice, retrieval and realistic pacing

A workable starting routine is **20–30 minutes on most learning days**, adjusted for attention, fatigue and available help. For example: 3–5 minutes of unaided retrieval, 8–12 minutes on one new/blocked model, 5–8 minutes of independent transfer, and a short spoken explanation or exit item. This is a planning suggestion, not a required daily minimum or a guaranteed completion timetable. Short successful sessions are preferable to a long sequence of guessing.

Use the following tentative review schedule:

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| End of the first session | One fresh number set, no displayed construction answer; ask what was preserved and why the units/sign/whole matter. |
| Next learning day | Retrieve the rule or model from memory before opening hints, then solve another representation. |
| About 3 days later | Mix the topic with two already learned topics so the learner must choose a method. |
| About 1 week later | Use a short new context or a diagram with different orientation; include a plausible wrong solution to explain/repair. |
| About 2–4 weeks later | Check an unfamiliar written question and revisit the weakest relation if it fails. |

If retrieval fails, offer the relevant model and worked example, then check a different case later. Do not mark the repaired, immediately revealed answer as independent mastery. If the learner retrieves and transfers comfortably, lengthen the interval and move to the next useful prerequisite/topic. Do not mechanically assign all 100 variations of one board before any other learning.

Save mistakes by misconception—for example, wrong whole, unequal parts, negative order, missing distribution, wrong height, wrong arc or unweighted mean—rather than treating every wrong answer as the same failure. The proposed route needs a richer model than just “completed screen.”

**Current recommendation helper:** `recommendedPracticeRoute` in `lib/learning/recommendations.dart` introduces **26 independent generated-round transfers**: 6 number-line rounds, 4 equal-group rounds, 4 sharing rounds, 4 fraction-tile rounds, then 1 each for balance, area, angles, similarity, volume, sets, data and probability. The number-line introduction prioritises three small sums before small nonnegative-result subtractions. ### Confirmed source issues and adaptation warnings

1. **Grade 7 p38, altitude example:** the narrative says 116 m below sea level; the diagram/table and solution use 120 m below. Using 4307−(−120) gives 4427 m, while the narrative value gives 4423 m. This was confirmed in the original PDF during the earlier source review. Do not import its mixed numbers into a generated answer key. The catalogue uses newly authored numbers instead.
2. **Grade 8 p55, square-root explanation:** the original PDF visibly prints `(−5)^5 = (5)^2 = 25`. The first exponent is a source typo, not merely a text-extraction problem: `(−5)^5=−3125`; the intended square comparison is `(−5)^2=(5)^2=25`. The accompanying statement that neither −5 nor 5 squares to −25 is correctly taught in the root blueprint. This page was rendered and inspected during this audit.
3. **Grade 7 final range:** the earlier “1–214” and chapter-7 end “214” were documentation mistakes. The original PDF’s last nonblank instructional page is printed 213 (PDF page219), followed by blank PDF page220. Those endpoints were corrected in the catalogue/JSON and validator; this is not an error in the book’s footer.
4. **Original extensions:** the catalogue’s negative-product distributive bridge, inverse-scaling fraction division, circle-sector rearrangement and explicit `√(x²)=|x|` contrast are mathematical teaching adaptations tied to source objectives. Do not present every animation or exact verbal derivation as a printed book activity. The source pages support the topic; the design supplies additional explanation.
5. **Diagram-sensitive statements:** Euclidean projections require matching `AC²=AB×AD` and `BC²=AB×DB` to a labelled altitude diagram. Grade 8 p132 was visually checked: the converse explicitly states the two identities with `CD⊥AB`. Inscribed-angle claims must select the same intercepted arc, and intersecting-chord angles use the **sum** of opposite arcs for an interior intersection. Broad source tags do not remove these conditions.
6. **Coverage claim:** 89 blueprints span the 15 chapters, but neither the catalogue nor the runtime establishes exhaustive syllabus coverage. Tangent/secant construction, specific quadrilateral constructions, all algebra/graph variants and contextual interpretation need further authoring. The supplied 2022 editions do not establish current exam topic weights, formats or readiness thresholds.

## English from zero literacy and science scope

The app now includes **one early printed-word recognition lab** for ONE, TWO, THREE and FOUR. Its initial study pairs each word with a numeral and actual dots. Its matching phase separates bare word cards from shuffled numeral/dot cards, requires all four correct pairs and permits visible error repair. The transfer asks `THREE=?`, answer 3. This is retrieval of a taught word, not evidence that the learner can decode a new English word. Reference mappings belong to initial study and optional hints; the outer matching goal must remain answer-free.

An English topic label, translation toggle or this small recognition exercise does not provide an English course from zero literacy. The current app has no pronunciation teaching, verified reading assessment, phonics sequence, listening comprehension course, handwriting/spelling practice or native-speaker recordings. **No audio is bundled for this lab.** Its source labels and answer checking cannot establish spoken pronunciation. Even reading numerals and mathematical symbols should be probed rather than assumed from being in Grade 7/8.

A separate English track would begin by checking oral language and print awareness, then teach listening/meaning, letter–sound relationships, blending/decoding, useful words, short sentences, supported reading and comprehension. Progress would require hearing a new word and choosing its meaning, decoding an unseen appropriate word, reading a short sentence and demonstrating understanding—not merely recalling a translated maths label. Use reviewed Afaan Oromo explanations and recordings to provide access while English develops. No English literacy textbook or official syllabus was supplied, so the exact scope, sequence and answer materials cannot be source-grounded from the mathematics books alone.

Likewise, money, plants, temperature or weather examples in mathematics do not constitute a science course. No science textbook, curriculum or practical-assessment requirements were supplied. A science track needs its own source map, concepts, vocabulary, diagrams, observations/experiments and transfer gates. Arithmetic, ratio, measurement, graphs and probability can support it, but science learning must be checked through scientific explanation and evidence. Do not count a crop array as a biology lesson or a token simulation as validated meteorology.

The current review therefore supports a mathematics prototype with representative actions, a small English printed-word recognition activity, a broader mathematics content design and a proposed route. A full English zero-literacy course, science coverage, reviewed Afaan Oromo/audio and current-exam alignment remain distinct content work.
